# Why I took this direction

## Who I am speaking to

The main audience is federal contractors and government hiring managers looking for someone who can turn reporting needs into useful dashboards and applications. My growing interest in UX belongs in the story, but it should not overshadow the experience I already bring.

## Start with the work

The home page says what I do and who I do it for. From there, visitors can open a case study, learn about my background, or reach me on LinkedIn. I kept the navigation short because those are the main things someone needs.

## Keep the visuals quiet

I chose a dark charcoal background with teal as an accent. Large headings create a clear reading order, while the lighter interface illustrations give the work a place to stand out. System fonts keep the site simple to load and maintain.

I was drawn to a stronger visual direction, but the page still needs to be easy to scan. It should feel like someone who builds for busy leaders made the choices.

## Explain my part

Each case study follows the same basic sequence: the problem, my approach, design decisions, and the result. The current role story goes further because I can explain the decisions from the original request through user acceptance testing.

For that work, the important choice was to look beyond copying SharePoint. I checked what data was available, looked for useful related information, and built a view that helped users get from an organization to the details they needed.

## Show the limits clearly

The illustrations are made for this portfolio. They are not screenshots or reconstructions of work systems. They help explain a general idea without exposing a customer’s application.

I have not added invented time savings or treated work in progress as a finished result. The Figma and Google UX projects will be added when there is actual work to show.

## What I would like to learn from visitors

Can someone explain what I do after reading the opening section? Can they tell what I personally contributed to the dashboard project? Can they find the case studies and contact link comfortably on a phone?

Those are questions for future usability sessions. I have not conducted those sessions yet, and this document is not a research report.
